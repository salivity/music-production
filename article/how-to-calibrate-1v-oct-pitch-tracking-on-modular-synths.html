How to Calibrate 1V/Oct Pitch Tracking on Modular Synths

Calibrating 1V/Octave (one volt per octave) control voltage (CV) pitch tracking ensures that an analog modular synthesizer oscillator plays in tune across multiple octaves. Because analog components drift with temperature and age, regular calibration aligns the oscillator's response to incoming CV with standard musical intervals. This guide outlines the essential tools, environmental preparation, and step-by-step procedure required to calibrate your Voltage Controlled Oscillators (VCOs) for reliable musical performance.

Required Tools and Equipment

To achieve accurate pitch tracking, you need the following items:

  • Accurate Tuner: A high-precision digital guitar tuner, strobe tuner, or a dedicated software tuner plugin capable of cent-level accuracy.
  • Precision CV Source: A thoroughly calibrated MIDI-to-CV converter, a digital sequencer, or an accurately scaled keyboard that outputs clean 1V increments.
  • Trimming Tool: A non-conductive ceramic screwdriver or an appropriately sized jeweler's flathead screwdriver to adjust the module's trimpots without shorting components.
  • High-Quality Patch Cables: Low-resistance cables to avoid unwanted voltage drops.

Preparation and Warm-Up

Analog oscillators rely on temperature-sensitive transistors. Before making adjustments:

  1. Warm Up the System: Power on the modular case and let the modules run for at least 20 to 30 minutes to reach internal operating temperature.
  2. Close the Enclosure: If the trimpots are accessible from the front panel, keep the case sealed. If trimpots are on the rear of the module, mount it securely on an extender or take care to calibrate in a room with minimal drafts.
  3. Bypass Modulation: Turn off all internal and external modulation sources. Set frequency modulation (FM), pulse-width modulation (PWM), and hard/soft sync to zero. Center all fine and coarse tune knobs.

The Two-Point Calibration Procedure

Most analog VCOs feature a main scaling trimpot (often labeled "V/OCT," "SCALE," or "TRIM") located on the circuit board or front panel. Calibration involves an iterative process of matching a lower base note and an upper target note.

Step 1: Send the Base Reference Voltage

Send a low-register note (such as C2, typically corresponding to 1V or 2V) from your precision CV source into the VCO's 1V/Oct input. Monitor the simple waveform output (preferably a triangle or sine wave) using your tuner. Use the front-panel Coarse/Fine Tune knob to tune this note exactly to pitch (e.g., C2 at 0 cents).

Step 2: Send the Upper Reference Voltage

Send a note three or four octaves higher (such as C5 or C6, adding exactly 3V or 4V) from your CV source. Observe the tuner:

  • If the pitch is sharp: The oscillator is over-tracking (the scale is too wide).
  • If the pitch is flat: The oscillator is under-tracking (the scale is too narrow).

Step 3: Adjust the Scale Trimpot

Use your trimming tool to adjust the V/Oct trimpot on the VCO:

  • If the high note is sharp, turn the trimpot so the pitch moves slightly downward.
  • If the high note is flat, turn the trimpot so the pitch moves slightly upward.

Note: Do not attempt to bring the high note fully in tune using only the trimpot in one go. Make small adjustments beyond or toward the target pitch, as moving the scale trimpot shifts the base note as well.

Step 4: Re-Tune and Iterate

  1. Switch back to the base note (C2).
  2. Use the front-panel Coarse/Fine Tune knob to bring the base note back to 0 cents.
  3. Switch back to the high note (C5/C6) to recheck the pitch.
  4. Repeat Steps 2 through 4 until both the low note and the high note register accurately at 0 cents on the tuner.

High-Frequency Compensation (HF Trim)

Some precision VCOs include a secondary trimpot labeled "HF" (High Frequency) or "COMP." At very high frequencies, analog transistors encounter physical limits that cause notes above C6 or C7 to track flat, even if the lower octaves are calibrated perfectly.

  1. Send a very high note (such as C6 or C7).
  2. If the note is flat despite proper 1V/Oct scaling in lower octaves, adjust the HF Trimpot until the high note hits 0 cents.
  3. Verify the lower octaves again to ensure the HF adjustment did not alter the lower range.

Best Practices for Long-Term Stability

  • Avoid Unbuffered Mults: When sending the same pitch CV to multiple oscillators, always use an active, buffered multiple to prevent voltage drops.
  • Re-Calibrate Seasonally: Significant shifts in room temperature and humidity will affect analog tracking; check calibration every few months or before critical recording sessions.
