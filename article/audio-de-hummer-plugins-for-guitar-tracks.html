Audio De-Hummer Plugins for Guitar Tracks

An audio de-hummer plugin targets and eliminates low-frequency electrical interference—specifically the pervasive 50 Hz or 60 Hz mains buzz and its associated harmonics—from live guitar recordings. This guide explains why live guitars are particularly susceptible to hum, how de-hummer plugins function to clean up recorded tracks, and how to apply them effectively without damaging the instrument's natural tone.

The Cause of Hum in Live Guitar Recordings

Electric guitars are prone to capturing unwanted electromagnetic interference (EMI) and radio frequency interference (RFI). This problem is most common when using instruments equipped with single-coil pickups, which act as antennas for nearby power sources, lighting rigs, computer monitors, and transformers.

Additionally, poor venue wiring or ground loops between amplifiers, pedalboards, and audio interfaces frequently inject steady, low-frequency hum into the signal chain. When high-gain pedals or amplifier distortion are applied, this low-level noise is drastically amplified, resulting in a loud buzz that distracts from the performance.

How an Audio De-Hummer Plugin Functions

A dedicated de-hummer plugin isolates and attenuates these electrical artifacts using specialized filtering techniques that outperform standard equalization:

  • Fundamental Frequency Targeting: Mains electricity operates at 60 Hz in North America and 50 Hz in Europe and other parts of the world. A de-hummer allows users to lock onto this exact fundamental frequency.
  • Harmonic Series Removal: Hum rarely stays confined to a single frequency; it manifests as a fundamental tone accompanied by a series of integer harmonics (e.g., 60 Hz, 120 Hz, 180 Hz, 240 Hz, and onward). De-hummers automatically calculate and link notch filters to suppress these harmonic overtones across the frequency spectrum.
  • Narrow Notch Filters (High Q): Unlike standard corrective EQ, which often uses broad curves that scoop out musical content, de-hummers deploy extremely steep, narrow notch filters. This removes the buzz while leaving adjacent musical frequencies intact.
  • Dynamic and Adaptive Tracking: Modern de-humming tools can analyze audio in real time, adapting the reduction level to the intensity of the noise. This ensures the filter only engages aggressively during quiet passages, rests, or sustained notes where the hum is exposed.

Benefits Over Standard Equalization

Using a standard multi-band parametric EQ to address hum usually requires manual placement of dozens of narrow notches. Even then, static EQ cuts can thin out the low-end warmth of a bass guitar or dull the midrange punch of an electric rhythm track.

A de-hummer provides linked control over filter slope, depth, and harmonic count, saving production time and preserving the body, transient punch, and low-end clarity of the instrument.

Best Practices for Applying De-Hummers

  1. Place Early in the Chain: Insert the de-hummer as the first plugin on the track. Removing the noise before the signal hits compressors, saturators, or amp simulators prevents the noise floor from being magnified later in the mix.
  2. Identify the Source Grid: Set the base frequency control correctly according to where the track was recorded (50 Hz vs. 60 Hz). Selecting the incorrect fundamental will leave the primary noise source untouched while removing useful frequencies.
  3. Use the Minimum Harmonics Necessary: Avoid removing high-order harmonics if they are inaudible in the full mix. Over-filtering upper frequencies can introduce phase smearing or make the guitar sound hollow.
  4. Utilize "Difference" or "Delta" Monitoring: Most professional de-hummer plugins feature a monitoring mode that outputs only the audio being removed. Use this feature to confirm that only the constant buzz is heard in isolation, ensuring no chords, fundamentals, or pick attacks are being stripped away.
