How to Sidechain Bass to Kick in Music Production

Sidechain compression is an essential mixing technique used to create space in the low end of a mix by automatically reducing the volume of the bass line whenever the kick drum hits. In modern music production, low frequencies carry the most acoustic energy, meaning an overlapping kick and sub-bass will quickly muddy a mix or cause unwanted clipping. By routing the kick drum's transient signal into a compressor placed on the bass channel, producers ensure both elements punch through clearly, maintaining low-end clarity, headroom, and a cohesive rhythmic groove.

The Routing Setup

To set up sidechain compression, start by loading a compressor that supports external sidechaining onto your bass track or bass bus. Next, navigate to the compressor's sidechain input menu and select your kick drum track as the external trigger source (often labeled "External" or "Key Input"). With this routing established, the compressor will evaluate the kick drum’s incoming volume instead of the bass itself, applying gain reduction to the bass only when the kick sounds.

Setting Threshold and Ratio

Begin by setting a moderate ratio between 2:1 and 4:1 for subtle transparency, or 6:1 and higher for aggressive electronic pumping effects. Play the track and lower the threshold until the kick triggers the compressor. For clean mixing, aim for approximately 3 dB to 6 dB of gain reduction during each kick hit. This amount clears enough headroom for the kick's fundamental frequency without causing the bass note to drop out entirely.

Tuning Attack and Release

The envelope settings dictate how natural or rhythmic the ducking effect feels:

  • Attack: Set the attack time as fast as possible (0.1 ms to 2 ms) to instantly catch the kick's initial transient. If the attack is too slow, the transient of the kick and the sub-frequencies of the bass will clash before the compressor reacts, resulting in a muddy thump or digital distortion.
  • Release: Adjust the release time to match the tempo of the track and the decay of the kick drum. The bass should return to full volume immediately after the kick drum finishes playing, but before the next transient arrives. A release set too fast will produce audible, jarring clicks, while a release set too slow will bury the bass line completely.

Using Filters and Ghost Triggers

Many modern compressors feature an internal sidechain high-pass or low-pass filter. Setting this filter ensures that only the punchy fundamental of the kick triggers the compressor, ignoring bleed from cymbals or snare hits on an acoustic drum track. In genres with long 808s or sustained acoustic kicks, producers often use a "ghost kick"—a short, muted click or transient sample routed exclusively to the sidechain input—to duck the bass quickly without waiting for a sustained kick body to decay.
