Differences Between VST, AU, and AAX Plugins

In digital audio production, plugins expand the capabilities of a Digital Audio Workstation (DAW) by providing virtual instruments, audio effects, and processing tools. This article breaks down the primary differences between the three standard plugin formats—VST, AU, and AAX—focusing on their development origins, operating system compatibility, supported DAWs, and technical performance in music production.

VST (Virtual Studio Technology)

Developed by Steinberg in 1996, Virtual Studio Technology (VST) is the most widely used and versatile plugin format in the audio industry.

  • Compatibility: Cross-platform (Windows, macOS, and Linux).
  • Supported DAWs: Cubase, Ableton Live, FL Studio, Studio One, Reaper, and many others.
  • Versions:
    • VST2: The legacy standard, largely phased out by major developers.
    • VST3: The current standard, introduced in 2008. It offers significant improvements, including dynamic CPU usage (deactivating processing when no audio is present), sample-accurate automation, flexible audio bus routing, and resizable user interfaces.

Because VST is an open standard, it boasts the largest ecosystem of third-party commercial and free plugins available today.

AU (Audio Units)

Audio Units (AU) is Apple’s proprietary plugin architecture, introduced with macOS to provide a system-level solution for audio processing.

  • Compatibility: macOS and iOS only.
  • Supported DAWs: Logic Pro, GarageBand, and macOS versions of third-party DAWs like Ableton Live and Studio One.
  • Key Features: Because AU is built directly into Apple's Core Audio architecture, it integrates tightly with the macOS operating system. This native integration often results in high stability, low latency, and efficient memory management on Apple hardware.

AU plugins are mandatory for producers who use Logic Pro, as Apple’s flagship DAW does not support VST or AAX natively.

AAX (Avid Audio eXtension)

Introduced with Pro Tools 10, AAX is a proprietary format created by Avid Technology to replace the older RTAS (Real-Time AudioSuite) and TDM (Time Division Multiplexing) formats.

  • Compatibility: Windows and macOS, but restricted strictly to Avid Pro Tools.
  • Supported DAWs: Pro Tools exclusively.
  • Sub-Formats:
    • AAX Native: Runs on the host computer’s central processor (CPU), similar to standard VST and AU plugins.
    • AAX DSP: Utilizes dedicated digital signal processing (DSP) chips on Pro Tools HDX hardware systems. This offloads processing from the computer, allowing for near-zero-latency monitoring and tracking in high-end studio environments.

AAX plugins undergo a strict licensing and certification process with Avid, which helps ensure high stability across professional recording workflows.

Choosing the Right Format

When installing plugins, your choice of format is determined by your operating system and primary DAW:

  • Choose AU if you work primarily in Logic Pro on macOS.
  • Choose AAX if you work in Pro Tools.
  • Choose VST3 if you use almost any other DAW (such as Ableton Live, FL Studio, or Studio One) on either Windows or macOS.
